# Anti-slop writing rules

Every skill that writes text a person will read applies these rules before the text leaves. That covers chat replies, form answers, proposals, emails, posts, case studies and reports.

Why it matters: readers now spot machine-written text fast, and once they do they stop trusting the rest. A study of 15 million PubMed abstracts found at least 13.5% of 2024 abstracts showed signs of LLM processing, detected purely from a jump in certain style words. If a word list can catch it, a hiring manager can too.

## The rule

Write like a specific person who knows the subject and is in a hurry. Plain verbs, real numbers, short sentences of different lengths. Say what you do not know.

## Banned patterns

| Pattern | Example of the problem | Write instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Em dash used as a pause | `The bot replies fast — and it never guesses.` | A comma or a full stop. |
| Negative parallelism | "It's not just a chatbot, it's a front desk." / "Not X, but Y." | Say the second half directly. |
| Reflex triplets | "fast, reliable, and scalable" | Keep the one or two that are true and provable. |
| Cliche vocabulary | delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, landscape, vibrant, robust, showcase, underscore, foster, seamless, leverage, elevate, unlock | is, has, uses, shows, builds, cuts, finds. |
| Importance padding | "plays a vital role", "stands as a", "serves as" | State what the thing does. |
| Tacked-on -ing clause | "..., ensuring quality across every step." | Make it a second sentence or delete it. |
| Vague authority | "experts say", "studies show", "many believe" | Name the source and link it, or cut the claim. |
| Stiff transitions | moreover, furthermore, additionally, in conclusion, overall | Start the next point. |
| Chatbot openers and closers | "Great question!", "Certainly!", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if you'd like me to..." | Open with the answer. Stop when done. |
| Formatting as decoration | bold in every line, bullets for two items, Title Case Headings, emoji | Sentence case. Bold only on labels. Bullets only for parallel items. |
| Rotating synonyms | "the tool... the solution... the platform... the system" for one thing | One name per thing, used every time. |
| Generic endings | "the future looks bright", "the possibilities are endless" | End on the last useful fact or a direct question. |
| Over-hedging | "may potentially help to possibly reduce" | One hedge, where the doubt is real. |

## Required patterns

- A number, name, date or example in most paragraphs.
- Sentences under 25 words on average, with some much shorter.
- Active voice by default: "I tested six failure cases", not "six failure cases were tested".
- Honest limits stated plainly: "tested on demo data, no paying client yet".
- Words the reader uses, not internal labels.

## Check before sending

1. Search the text for the em dash character (`—`). Replace every one.
2. Search for each word in the cliche list. Replace or cut.
3. Read the first sentence alone. Does it answer or say something specific? If not, rewrite it.
4. Count bold phrases. More than one per paragraph is too many.
5. Read the last sentence alone. If it offers more help or says something generic, delete it.
