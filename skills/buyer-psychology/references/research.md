# Research notes: buyer-psychology

## Findings from the 8 Nov 2026 run that shaped the method

- Decision drivers for a no-review freelancer: work samples close to the client's project, a small paid first milestone, a narrow specific offer, clear written communication, signs the proposal was written for this client. Sources: https://www.upwork.com/resources/how-to-choose-freelancers and https://snipework.com/blog/first-upwork-job-2026
- AI-specific fears: wrong answers the business is bound to (Air Canada), invented prices (Chevrolet $1 Tahoe; a BMW dealer later honored a bot's offer and paid about US$5,000 more than it thought the car was worth), customers trapped with no human (77% have looked up ways around bots, ServiceNow n=2,000), brand blame (50% of 1,000 US consumers blame business leadership, Kim.cc), and silent failure. Sources: https://www.cxtoday.com/conversational-ai/court-orders-air-canada-to-pay-out-for-chatbots-bad-advice/ , https://letsdatascience.com/news/chevrolet-chatbot-accepts-1-tahoe-offer-973398ee , https://www.channeldive.com/news/3-numbers-on-the-cost-of-ai-failures/823822/ and https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/so-you-introduced-an-ai-chatbot-for-customers-heres-why-they-probably-hate-it
- Inaccuracy is the top AI concern for UK SMEs (25%, Bionic survey of 500). Source: https://hrreview.co.uk/hr-news/future-of-work-hr-news/ai-concerns-among-uk-smes/382175
- The gap found in that run: nothing covered "bot tricked into rudeness". The fix path (a short abuse test) became the "smallest proof" step.
