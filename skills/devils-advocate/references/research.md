# Research notes: devils-advocate

## Findings used

- Gary Klein's premortem (HBR, 2007): the team assumes the project failed and lists why. Klein cites Mitchell, Russo and Pennington (1989) for a 30% gain, but that study measured a 30% increase in the number of reasons generated, not their accuracy. Gallop and Bischoff (2016), with 101 experienced program managers and engineers, found premortems surfaced more quality risks than brainstorming. Evidence that it improves real project outcomes is thin. Source: https://corporate.jcx.au/premortem (course notes summarising the papers, not the papers themselves).
- That is why the skill reports confidence and treats its output as a list of things to check, not a prediction.
